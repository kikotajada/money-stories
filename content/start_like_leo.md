# Start Like Leo — Free Guide

Oct 22, 2026 · @Francisco Tajada Lostao

## Start here

Ten money moves for your first year in Switzerland, each one doable in an evening, that save most expats thousands of francs.

This guide is for you if you've just moved to Switzerland, or have been here a while and still don't quite get your payslip, your health insurance or your tax. It's free, short and doesn't sell you any product.

- **✗ Leo** arrived in Zurich last year. He made every one of these mistakes so you don't have to.
- **✓ Sofia**, his colleague, got them right. Do what she does.

Go in order: moves 1 to 6 protect you, moves 7 to 10 build your future. Tick each one off on the checklist at the end.

## Move 1 — Read your payslip once, properly

The gap between your gross and net salary is mostly your own future money, not lost money, so it's worth knowing what each line is.

| Line on your payslip | What it is | Roughly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AHV/IV/EO | State pension, disability and income-loss insurance | 5.3% of salary |
| ALV | Unemployment insurance | 1.1% (up to a salary cap) |
| NBU | Non-work accident insurance | Varies by employer, often around 1% |
| BVG / Pensionskasse | Your pension fund (2nd pillar), rises with age | Varies by employer and age |
| Quellensteuer | Income tax, if you're taxed at source | Depends on canton, income and family |

**Leo** assumed the pension fund line was a fee. **Sofia** asked HR for her pension fund certificate in her first week (move 7).

## Move 2 — Know how you're taxed

Most foreign residents without a C permit have income tax taken straight from their salary (Quellensteuer). That's convenient, but it doesn't include most personal deductions, such as pillar 3a.

- If you earn more than CHF 120,000 a year, you must file a full tax return anyway.
- If you earn less, you can ask for a full assessment by **31 March** of the following year to claim deductions like 3a. Once you ask, you usually keep filing every year.
- Your canton may also require a return if you have savings or other income above its limits (in Zurich: assets above CHF 80,000 or other income above CHF 3,000).

**Leo** didn't know he could claim his 3a and lost the deduction for a whole year. **Sofia** put 31 March in her calendar.

## Move 3 — Pick your health insurance on purpose

Basic health insurance is mandatory within three months of arriving, and two choices drive what you pay: the deductible (franchise) and the model.

- **Franchise:** for adults it ranges from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 a year. A higher franchise means a lower monthly premium but more out of pocket if you're ill. After the franchise you pay 10% of costs, up to CHF 700 a year.
- **Model:** standard (any doctor), family doctor, telemedicine or HMO. The restricted models are cheaper.
- **Switching:** to change insurer or lower your franchise for next year, your written notice must arrive by 30 November; raising the franchise can wait until 31 December.

**Leo** kept the CHF 300 franchise the broker suggested on day one, rarely saw a doctor, and overpaid about CHF 1,500 a year. **Sofia**, who is healthy, chose a high franchise and a telemedicine model, and keeps the franchise amount in her emergency fund.

## Move 4 — Build an emergency fund

Keep three to six months of spending in a separate savings account before you invest a single franc, plus your health-insurance franchise.

For Leo, who spends about CHF 4,500 a month, that's CHF 13,500 to CHF 27,000. It covers a job gap, a dentist bill or a sudden move without touching investments or borrowing.

**Leo** kept everything in one account and spent his "savings" without noticing. **Sofia** opened a second account, named it "don't touch" and set a standing order on payday.

## Move 5 — Handle the rent deposit the Swiss way

A Swiss landlord can ask for up to three months' rent as a deposit, and it must sit in a blocked account in your name, not in the landlord's pocket.

- The bank opens a rent-deposit account; the money stays yours and is released when both sides sign, or by the bank if the landlord makes no claim within a year after you move out.
- A deposit insurance (guarantee) avoids tying up cash, but you pay a yearly premium you never get back. Compare the cost over the years you expect to stay.
- At move-out, attend the handover inspection, take photos and get a copy of the report.

**Leo** paid the deposit by bank transfer straight to the landlord and waited months to get it back. **Sofia** insisted on a blocked account in her name.

## Move 6 — Stop paying hidden bank and currency fees

Account fees, card fees and especially currency conversion can quietly cost several hundred francs a year.

- Check your account's monthly fee and what's included.
- Paying in euros with a Swiss card often costs 1.5–2.5% in conversion and fees. A low-cost multi-currency account or card can cut that to a fraction.
- Sending money home: compare the exchange rate you actually get with the market rate, not just the transfer fee.

**Leo** sent CHF 1,000 a month to his family through his bank and lost about CHF 20 each time. **Sofia** compared three options once and switched.

## Move 7 — Read your pension fund certificate

Your pension fund certificate shows how much you've saved in the 2nd pillar, how much your employer adds, and whether you can make voluntary buy-ins that reduce your tax.

Ask HR or your fund's online portal for it. Look at three lines: your current savings, the employer and employee contributions, and the maximum buy-in. Buy-ins are tax-deductible but locked for three years and come with rules, so read them before you pay anything in.

If you leave for an EU/EFTA country, the mandatory part usually stays in a Swiss vested benefits account until retirement; outside the EU/EFTA you can generally take it all.

**Leo** had never opened the envelope. **Sofia** checks it every January.

## Move 8 — Know your AHV gaps

A full AHV state pension needs 44 years of contributions, so arriving at 30 means about 10 missing years and a smaller pension, roughly 2.3% less for each year missing.

- Order your free account statement (IK-Auszug) from your AHV compensation office or online to see your contribution years.
- Years you lived abroad before coming aren't Swiss gaps you can fill; years in EU/EFTA countries count towards those countries' pensions.
- Real gaps while you lived in Switzerland (for example, a year between jobs without contributions) can be paid back only for the last five years.

**Leo** assumed the state pension would be enough. **Sofia** knows hers will be smaller, which is exactly why she uses moves 9 and 10.

## Move 9 — Open your pillar 3a

Paying into pillar 3a, up to CHF 7,258 in 2026 with a pension fund, cuts your taxable income by the same amount and can save you well over CHF 1,000 a year.

Choose between a 3a interest account (safe, low return) and a 3a securities solution (invested, can grow and fall). Compare fees, not slogans. Don't confuse it with a 3b life-insurance policy: that's a different product with no federal tax deduction.

**Leo** signed a 3b policy by mistake. **Sofia** opened a low-fee 3a and set a monthly standing order of about CHF 605.

## Move 10 — Start investing (simply)

Once moves 1 to 9 are done, invest a fixed amount every month in a broad, low-cost world fund: it's the move that makes the biggest difference over 20 or 30 years.

At an assumed 5% a year, CHF 500 a month becomes about CHF 409,000 after 30 years, against about CHF 194,000 in a savings account at 0.5%.
